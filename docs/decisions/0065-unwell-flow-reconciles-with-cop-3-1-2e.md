## Context
Two published texts and one project rule meet on the "I feel unwell" sheet.
- The Technical Guideline tells the first aider to "notify supervisor". CoP 3.1.2(e) goes further: it makes reporting to the supervisor "immediately" a **duty of the employee**, for their own symptoms and for fellow employees'.
- Our rule (G9, ADR 0004, ADR 0054): no symptom report reaches a supervisor or employer dashboard, and the server holds no individual health data.
- The build today has only Call 998, `Site` has no phone fields, and the sheet says "Your supervisor does not see this screen." That is true of the product, but it can be read as "you need not tell your supervisor", which is the opposite of 3.1.2(e) (review pack Q1).
- 3.1.2(e) sends the report "as per Element 07 - Monitoring, Investigation and Reporting". That document has **not been read** for this project, so the reporting channel it prescribes is unverified.

## Options considered
| Option | Evidence | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| A message from the app to the supervisor dashboard | The server would hold a symptom report about a worker or crew, against ADR 0004 (no individual health information on the server) and ADR 0054 (no subject stored; health data refused to every role) and rule G9; workers have no account, so the sender could not be authenticated; HANDOFF decision 2 option (c) was rejected for the same reason | Rejected |
| A message from the app to a site first-aider role only (HANDOFF decision 2 option (a)) | Satisfies G9, but still stores a health message on the server, addressed to a role the product cannot authenticate (no worker or first-aider accounts, ADR 0054); a call reaches the same person with nothing stored | Not chosen (the architect's reading; Ali's choice below is HANDOFF option (b) plus a supervisor row) |
| 998 only (what is built today) | Offers no way to meet the supervisor duty in 3.1.2(e) and §4.5(c)(i); the sheet would address the ambulance alone and, with the "does not see this screen" line, nudge a worker to treat 998 as the whole duty | Rejected: not compliant with the intent of 3.1.2(e) |
| Three one-tap CALL actions (supervisor, site first-aider, 998); nothing logged or sent (chosen) | The phone call is the "notify": the supervisor learns by voice, as TG §4.5(c)(i) and 3.1.2(e) read; nothing about the worker's condition touches the server, so ADR 0004, ADR 0054 and G9 hold unchanged | Accepted |

## Decision
1. The "I feel unwell" sheet offers **three one-tap CALL actions** (`tel:` links): the supervisor, the site first-aider, and 998 (ambulance; "998 for ambulance", u.ae handling-emergencies page, opened 2026-11-14). The two site numbers come from `Site.supervisor_phone` and `Site.first_aider_phone`; **on the demo both are synthetic** (the SIMULATED fixture of ADR 0057). The order of the rows and which call is the hero are **not decided here**: the review pack asks the consultant (Q1 question 2, C2 question 2). Until the answer, the F2 plan's 998 hero stands.
2. **Nothing is logged to any dashboard, nothing is sent from the app, and no health data reaches the employer through the app.** A tap makes no request, writes no row and emits no analytics event; the timer state stays on the device (F2 Task 18 `test_timer_state_never_leaves_the_device`). The employer's own recording of an incident (CoP 3.1.1(l), 4(a)(v)) happens outside the product.
3. A row whose number is not set is not a dead button. It shows the empty-state line already designed for the first-aider row ("Ask the nearest person to call your first aider. No first aider number is set for this site."), worded the same way for the supervisor row (text to be written and reviewed).
4. The line "Your supervisor does not see this screen." must not stand as the only statement about the supervisor, because it can read as permission not to report. The review pack proposes "This app tells no one. Call your supervisor or first aider yourself." (Q1 question 3) and asks the consultant; the replacement is **not settled here**. Interim wording for the 18 Nov freeze, in case no reply arrives by 17 Nov, is open (the architect decides; the label stays "Not clinically reviewed").

## Consequences
- **Build, with no task on file.** `Site` gains two optional phone fields: an expand-only migration on the B3 `site` table (Task 7), the fixture `demo_site.toml`, the `Site` schema of `/api/v1` and the OpenAPI contract that F2 consumes; F2 Task 18 gains a supervisor row, and the first-aider row can leave DESIGNED. Neither plan has a task or an estimate for this. The architect must add one before the 18 Nov 18:00 freeze, or the rows stay DESIGNED (labels per ADR 0045 decision 5). Changing the fixture changes `demo_site_sha256` (ADR 0057 Consequences, pre-registration §11): the architect must check which side of the freeze addendum this lands on.
- **Gain.** No symptom data on the server; the worker has a one-tap way to meet 3.1.2(e); the claim "no health data reaches the employer through the app" stays true and testable.
- **Costs.** The supervisor learns only if the worker or a crew mate calls; the product keeps no record that a notification happened (the employer's recording duties stay the employer's). A phone without signal cannot call (review pack F5, unanswered). Three more safety strings per language need the native review that review pack Q4 says has not happened.
- **Demo hazard, flagged, not decided here.** `tel:` links dial for real on a phone. 998 is a live emergency number, and the two synthetic site numbers must not ring a real person; the choice of numbers needs a check against the UAE numbering plan, which was not done. The 998 hero is already in the F2 plan.
- ADR 0004's last Consequences bullet (an unwell message queued in IndexedDB) is now answered for the demo: the sheet sends nothing, so no queue is built. The bullet stays as history.
- **Cost of being wrong.** If the consultant, or a pilot employer's own protocol, says a call alone is not an acceptable "notify", the sheet needs an in-app report to a named role. That is a new ADR, a server table of health messages with PDPL and DoH questions, and the claim above is withdrawn. If we wrongly built the message instead, a worker's symptom report would sit on a supervisor dashboard: a privacy breach that cannot be undone and would teach workers to hide symptoms (G9). The asymmetry is why the call is the default.

## Revisit trigger
The consultant's review of Q1 (due 2026-11-17) says a call is not enough; a pilot employer's heat-illness protocol, or Element 07, requires an in-app report; or worker accounts exist (ADR 0054's trigger).
